Immigration

We help clients apply for permanent residency, seek protection, and pursue visas and citizenship in the United States.

We help clients apply for permanent residency in the United States. If approved, you will receive a “Green Card,” which allows you to live and work in the U.S. permanently. You may qualify for a Green Card in one of the following ways:

Family Based Petition | Asylum | Non‑Immigrant Visas | Special Immigrant Juvenile (SIJ) | Naturalization

Family Based Petition

U.S. citizens and permanent residents can apply for their close family members to get a Green Card and live in the United States. This process is called a family‑based petition. To qualify, the person applying must show that they have a real family relationship with the person they are sponsoring.

Two main steps:

  • Filing the Petition – The U.S. citizen or permanent resident files Form I‑130 (Petition for Alien Relative) to prove the family relationship.
  • Applying for a Green Card – Once the petition is approved and a visa is available, the family member applies for permanent residence.

Support & Eligibility

You must also show that you can support your family member financially. Some of the family members who may qualify include:

  • Spouses
  • Children (unmarried and under 21)
  • Parents (if the sponsor is over 21)
  • Brothers and sisters (for U.S. citizens only)

We help families understand the process, prepare the right forms, and support them every step of the way.

Asylum

If you are in the U.S. or arriving here and fear returning to your home country due to threats or harm, you may be eligible to apply for asylum. Asylum is a form of protection that allows you to stay in the U.S. and possibly become a permanent resident.

Eligibility Requirements

  • Physically present in the U.S.
  • Not a U.S. citizen
  • Fear returning to your home country due to persecution or a well‑founded fear of persecution based on:
    • Race
    • Religion
    • Nationality
    • Membership in a particular social group
    • Political opinion

Application Process

  • File Form I‑589 within one year of arrival, unless you qualify for an exception.
  • Provide supporting documents such as personal statements and country condition reports.
  • Attend an interview with a USCIS asylum officer or a hearing before an immigration judge.

Including Family Members

You can include your spouse and unmarried children under 21 years old in your asylum application if they are physically present in the U.S.

Work Authorization & Residency

If you are granted asylum, you may apply for work authorization and, after one year, for a Green Card.

Important Notes

You must file your asylum application within one year of your arrival in the U.S., unless you qualify for an exception. Certain criminal convictions may bar you from receiving asylum. If you are in removal proceedings, you may still apply, but the process will be handled by an immigration judge.

We can assist you in understanding the asylum process and help you prepare your application to ensure the best chance of success.

Non‑Immigrant Visas

Non‑immigrant visas are for people who want to visit the United States for a short time. These visas are for specific reasons and do not allow permanent stay.

Common Reasons

  • Tourism or visiting family
  • Studying at a school
  • Temporary work
  • Business trips
  • Medical treatment
  • Cultural or exchange programs

Visa Examples

  • B‑1/B‑2 visitor visas
  • F‑1 student visas
  • H‑1B work visas
  • J‑1 exchange visitor visas

Application Process

  • Fill out visa application forms.
  • Attend an interview at a U.S. embassy or consulate.
  • Provide proof of your reason to visit.
  • Show ties to your home country to prove you will return.

We help you understand and complete the visa process to increase your chance of approval.

Naturalization

Naturalization is the process for people born outside the United States to become U.S. citizens. After becoming a citizen, you have the same rights as those born in the U.S., such as voting and getting a U.S. passport.

Who Can Apply?

  • Have a Green Card and have lived in the U.S. for 3 to 5 years.
  • Are 18 years or older.
  • Have good moral character.
  • Can read, write, and speak basic English.
  • Pass a test about U.S. history and government.
  • Meet residency requirements.

Steps to Become a Citizen

  • Fill out and send Form N‑400 (Application for Naturalization).
  • Go to a fingerprint appointment.
  • Attend an interview with a USCIS officer.
  • Pass the English and civics tests.
  • Take the Oath of Allegiance.

Benefits of Citizenship

  • Vote in elections.
  • Get a U.S. passport.
  • Apply for government jobs.
  • Sponsor family members to live in the U.S.
  • Protection from deportation.

We help with the application, prepare you for the tests, and support you at the interview.

Special Immigrant Juvenile (SIJ)

Our office proudly assists children and young people who have been abused, abandoned, or neglected in applying for Special Immigrant Juvenile (SIJ) status. This immigration benefit can offer protection from deportation and provide a path to a Green Card.

Eligibility Requirements

  • Under 21 years old.
  • Not married.
  • Abused, abandoned, or neglected by one or both parents.
  • A state court has declared you dependent on the court or placed you in custody of a guardian or child services agency.
  • The court has found that you cannot live with at least one parent due to abuse, neglect, or abandonment.
  • The court has decided that it is not in your best interest to return to your home country.

Juvenile Court Order

You must first go to a state family or juvenile court. The judge must place you in custody of a guardian, agency, or the court, find that you cannot live with at least one parent due to abuse, neglect, or abandonment, and find that it is not in your best interest to return to your home country.

Form I‑360 (SIJ Petition)

You must file Form I‑360 with USCIS before you turn 21. It must include the court order and documents explaining the judge’s findings, proof of your age, and English translations of any non‑English documents.

Form I‑485 (Green Card Application)

Once your I‑360 is approved and a visa number is available, you can apply for a Green Card by filing Form I‑485. In some cases, you may file this at the same time as Form I‑360.

Our office helps children and teens with each step of the SIJ process, ensuring forms are filed correctly and on time.

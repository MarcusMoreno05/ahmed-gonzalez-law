You may qualify for a Green Card in one of the following ways: Family Based Petition | Asylum | Non‑Immigrant Visas | SIJ | Naturalization

Family Based Petition

U.S. citizens and permanent residents can apply for their close family members to get a Green Card.

Two steps:

  • File Form I‑130 to prove the family relationship.
  • Apply for a Green Card once the petition is approved.

Eligible relatives include:

  • Spouses
  • Children (unmarried, under 21)
  • Parents (if the sponsor is 21+)
  • Siblings (U.S. citizens only)

Asylum

If you fear returning to your home country due to persecution, you may qualify for asylum.

Eligibility:

  • Physically present in the U.S.
  • Fear persecution based on race, religion, nationality, social group, or political opinion.

Process:

  • File Form I‑589 within one year of arrival.
  • Provide supporting documents.
  • Attend an interview or hearing.

Important Notes

Some criminal convictions may affect eligibility. If you are in removal proceedings, asylum is handled by an immigration judge.

Non‑Immigrant Visas

Short‑term visas for tourism, study, work, business, or medical care.

Examples:

  • B‑1/B‑2 visitor visas
  • F‑1 student visas
  • H‑1B work visas
  • J‑1 exchange visas

Naturalization

Become a U.S. citizen after meeting residency and eligibility requirements.

Requirements:

  • Green Card for 3–5 years
  • 18+ years old
  • Good moral character
  • English & civics test

Special Immigrant Juvenile (SIJ)

For children under 21 who were abused, abandoned, or neglected.

Requirements:

  • Under 21
  • Not married
  • Juvenile court findings
  • Not in best interest to return home

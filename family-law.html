Child Support

Caring Approach to Child Support

Child support is a key part of ensuring your child’s financial needs are met during and after a divorce. Once custody is determined, child support must be addressed according to New York law. Calculating the right amount can be complex and emotional, and any deviation from the legal guidelines must be properly documented to avoid complications.

How Is Child Support Determined in New York?

New York calculates child support using a formula set by the Child Support Standards Act (CSSA). This formula determines the percentage of the non‑custodial parent’s income that should be paid toward the financial support of their child or children.

Basic CSSA Percentages

  • 17% of income for 1 child
  • 25% for 2 children
  • 29% for 3 children
  • 31% for 4 children
  • At least 35% for 5 or more children

These percentages apply to the combined parental income up to $143,000. The non‑custodial parent usually pays child support.

What Counts as Income?

The court considers many sources of income, including:

  • Workers’ compensation
  • Pensions and annuities
  • Disability and unemployment benefits
  • Social Security and veterans benefits

Public assistance is excluded from income calculations. We help you understand how the law applies to your situation and work to ensure child support is fair and appropriate.

Trusts and Wills

Estate planning helps protect your family and property. Trusts and wills are key tools to ensure your wishes are followed.

Trusts

A trust is a legal arrangement where a grantor transfers assets to a trustee to manage for the benefit of one or more beneficiaries. Reasons to create a trust include avoiding probate, reducing taxes, and managing assets in case of illness.

Benefits of a Trust

  • Privacy and avoiding public probate proceedings.
  • Continuity in managing assets if you become ill or incapacitated.
  • Asset protection and control over how and when beneficiaries receive property.
  • Flexibility to address complex family or financial situations.

Wills

A Last Will and Testament ensures your property is distributed according to your wishes and avoids intestacy laws. It allows you to appoint executors, guardians, and minimize family disputes.

Benefits of Having a Will

  • Specific gifts to loved ones or charities.
  • Guardianship plans for minor children.
  • Instructions for pet care.
  • Ability to create testamentary trusts.

We help you understand your options and prepare documents that reflect your values and goals.
